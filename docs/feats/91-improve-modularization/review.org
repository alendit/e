The remediation fixes the three previously reported contracts:

1. Both settlement follow-up receiver methods now carry the caller's exact
   attached-turn port through authorization and through queue/publication.
   A rejected stale port cannot borrow the active entry's replacement port, and
   rejection occurs before either side effect.

   exact client/observer lease remains live and another page remains.  Callback
   fault, detach, stale/cancelled receiver, unsubscribe, and board close all
   terminate; healthy five-message/two-message-page fixtures return
   =(t t nil)= for both public pumps.
3. The local dependency evidence now defines an all-literal recursive scan,
   includes all four optional local loads, and reproducibly reports 163 modules,
   709 unique edges, and no cycle.

The broader physical decomposition remains sound.  Presentation, session,
harness/runtime, OpenAI, MCP, base, and context owners retain the direction and
lifetime conclusions from the first audit, and the full repository suite is
green at this revision.

Fixed point is nevertheless blocked by the new stale-binding cleanup defect.
When the binding's main observer is stale while its board remains active,
=e-chat-service-drain-binding= calls a retirement helper whose old precondition
was a non-live board.  It detaches the main client but leaves the binding in the
harness/session catalog and leaves independent subscriber clients attached.
The next binding lookup returns that detached object, and the next submit fails
with =stale-requester= instead of rebuilding or otherwise presenting a coherent
terminal state.

No credentialed provider call was needed for this behavior-neutral topology
review.  External OpenAI/MCP provider capability is unconfirmed/not applicable;
it is not being counted as preservation evidence.

* Findings

** DONE Medium: Stale attached-turn receiver bypassed its own authorization

*** Problem

At =0f4bc1c6=, the two port receiver methods discarded their receiver and the
inner settlement operations rediscovered the active entry's port.  A rejected
stale port could therefore queue through or publish with a valid replacement,
violating the one-attachment receiver fence recorded by =F91-RUNTIME=.

*** Change

At =c55ffd5e4=, both wrappers pass =:attached-turn-port port=, both inner
operations derive the token from that exact receiver, and
=e-harness-turn--require-attached-port= authorizes and returns the supplied
object.  Queueing uses that returned port and publication invokes its publisher.
No replacement-port fallback or mutating side effect occurs after rejection.

*** Evidence

=test/e-harness-runtime-owner-test.el:193-252= proves both negative and positive
paths.  The direct reproduction authorized the stale receiver twice, received
two =e-harness-board-attachment-required= errors, recorded zero queued prompts,
and invoked neither publisher.  The isolated owner suite passed 9/9 and full ERT
passed.

** DONE Medium: Faulted public drain reported work that could never progress

*** Problem

At =0f4bc1c6=, a callback fault retired its subscription without accepting the
page, after which the public wrapper compared only the unchanged cursor with the
board tail and returned =t=.  The documented synchronous =while= contract could
spin forever.  Equivalent stale/detached/closing states were not represented in
the public return precondition.

*** Change

At =c55ffd5e4=, the public drains require an active exact client/observer lease
before invoking the bounded drain and recheck it before reporting another page.
Subscription teardown records a terminal state and returns nil.  Binding,
subscription, callback-fault, detach, stale/cancelled, unsubscribe, and closing
fixtures all terminate, while healthy multi-page fixtures retain true progress.

*** Evidence

close, and unsubscribe assertions, including bounded =while= use.  The focused
suite passed 38/38.  Independent fixtures returned nil for every requested

** TODO Medium: Stale active-board drain leaves a zombie binding and subscriber client

*** Expected

The drain's new terminal semantics must leave service-owned process state
coherent.  =F91-GOAL= and =F91-RUNTIME= require a usable public composition and
one correctly fenced owner/lifetime; AGENTS.md requires mutable state to have
one owner and unexpected failures to surface at a boundary that can make the
domain decision.  A terminal stale receiver may be repaired or invalidated, but
later =ensure-binding=/submit must not receive a detached object still cataloged
as live, and retiring a binding must not orphan its independent clients.

*** Observed

For an active board with a cancelled main observer,
=e-chat-service-drain-binding= takes the new branch at
=lisp/core/e-chat-service.el:1393-1398= and calls
=e-chat-service--retire-binding=.  That helper, lines 233-251, marks subscription
objects inactive, removes the binding only from the board-to-bindings map, and
detaches only the main client.  It neither removes the harness/session entry nor
detaches the independent subscription clients.  Because
=e-chat-service-binding= at lines 284-292 defines liveness only by the still-
active registered board, it returns the same retired binding.

A fresh synthetic fixture added one independent subscriber, cancelled only the
main observer, drained once, looked up the binding, and submitted again:

#+begin_example
(:drain nil
 :same-binding t
 :main-client-state detached
 :main-client-current nil
 :subscription-active nil
 :subscriber-client-state active
 :subscriber-client-current t
 :submit
 (e-board-registry-authorization-denied
  "e board registry authorization denied: ... stale-requester"))
#+end_example

The new test at =test/e-modernchat-test.el:1519-1560= proves that the loop stops
but does not assert catalog invalidation, independent-client release, or a usable
subsequent binding.  Its close assertion also reuses the binding already retired
by the stale-observer half, so the separate review fixture was needed to verify
a live binding entering =closing=.

*** Why it matters

The public drain now terminates, but it converts one stale presentation cursor
into a broken session service object.  Normal later service calls reuse a client
that the same retirement just detached and fail authorization.  Independent
subscriber clients remain active in the board registry although their
subscriptions were made inactive and removed from the service list.  That is a
cross-owner lifetime leak and a recovery failure hidden by the green full suite.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Give the active-board stale-binding branch one coherent lifecycle outcome.
Either rehabilitate/replace the service-owned observer without retiring the
binding, or atomically invalidate the binding from every service catalog and
release every owned subscription client so the next ensure operation can build
a usable binding.  Preserve the durable board/session association and do not
add a fallback or compatibility path.  Add a regression that cancels the main
observer while the board is active, includes an independent subscriber, asserts
the first drain returns nil, then proves catalog/client cleanup and a successful
subsequent ensure/submit.  Keep the board-closing case independent.  Rerun the
chat-service/board-runtime consumers, preservation selector, and full ERT.

process-local execution state in harness/board runtime, request/transport state
in provider/MCP/Work adapters, and buffer/window state in presentation owners.
Storage, provider/network, process, board publication, and display side effects

The complete 709-edge local graph is acyclic.  Facades depend on owners; owners
do not call their facades.  Session value owners do not depend on aggregate or
storage, context lifetime points to the lower estimator, MCP capability is
transport-independent, and Bash does not depend on the file owner.

The public seams remain narrow and consumer-shaped: detached board envelope,
semantic continuation reconciliation, default harness projection, attachment
live buffer, validated session-tmp path, exact attached-turn receiver, and one-
page chat drains.  The stale-binding issue does not justify another generic
abstraction.  It requires one explicit service lifecycle decision and matching
cleanup/recovery postconditions.
