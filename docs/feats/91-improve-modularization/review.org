subscription is authorized.  After grouping, however, the classifier bucket
retains only participant and subscription ids.  The prepare and commit phases

** TODO Medium: Runtime terminal cleanup is not idempotent for invocations and pickup drains

*** Expected behavior

=F91-RUNTIME= and the current architecture describe
=e-board-runtime-retire-attachment= as the one exact terminal owner for
attachment-local invocation, FIFO, callback, and quiescence state.  Unexpected
callback errors should surface while the same public operation remains
retryable, and an old callback must not become authority for a replacement
attachment.

*** Observed behavior

=e-board-runtime--drop-invocation= at
=lisp/core/e-board-runtime.el:913-918= decrements unsettled accounting before
removing the invocation.  The decrement calls fallible notification hooks.  A
notification signal therefore leaves the invocation present at count 0; retry
decrements it to -1 and signals before removal.  Separately,
=e-board-runtime--apply-invocation-effect= at lines 1296-1324 marks an
invocation =applying=, calls arbitrary owner behavior, and then decrements.  If
that callback reentrantly retires the attachment, retirement also drops and
decrements the applying invocation; the outer path decrements it a second time.

=e-board-runtime--enqueue-pickups= and =--drain-pickups= at lines 1541-1591
use the global =e-board-runtime--pickup-drain-scheduled= boolean without a
lifetime generation.  Attachment retirement removes exact queue cells at
lines 1107-1121 but does not reconcile that scheduled authority.  A retained
old callback can keep the boolean true after the queue becomes empty.  New
replacement work then queues without scheduling a current callback, and the
old callback can later drain and deliver the new generation.

*** Evidence

The independent probes produced these exact states:

#+begin_example
notification fault: invocation present, count 0
retry:             invocation present, count -1, negative-count error

reentrant apply:   attachment stage done, invocation count -1

replacement pickup queued: pending true, newly scheduled callbacks 0,
                           deliveries 0
after old callback:         deliveries 1
#+end_example

The activity owner is a useful control: its generation-fenced stale drain is a
no-op and a newly scheduled drain preserves another attachment.  Producer,
reconciliation, and activity callback-fault probes also remain retryable, so
this is localized to invocation transition order and pickup scheduler lifetime.

*** Why it matters

A negative invocation counter makes quiescence state invalid and prevents the
terminal owner from proving cleanup.  A replacement pickup can stall forever
if the hostile/stopped scheduler never invokes the old callback; if it does,
an old lifetime's callback performs the new lifetime's work.  Both violate the
advertised exact, retryable terminal contract even though common-path ERT is
green.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Make invocation settlement/removal one idempotent owner transition before any
fallible notification, and coordinate the =applying= path with retirement so
exactly one side owns the terminal decrement.  Preserve error propagation, but
do not leave a state that a retry can decrement again.  Give pickup drains a
generation/receipt like activity drains (or an equivalent exact scheduled
authority), clear/fence it when retirement removes the queue item, and schedule
fresh remaining/replacement work independently.  Add direct notification-fault,
reentrant-apply/retirement, repeated-completion, and hostile old/new pickup
scheduler regressions.

** TODO Medium: Grouped classifiers lose the exact subscription lifetime fence

*** Expected behavior

=F91-OWNER=, =F91-RUNTIME=, and the current architecture/audit claim that the
board-monotonic subscription token fences every queued classifier phase and
prevents old route work from affecting a same-id participant/subscription or
attachment replacement.

*** Observed behavior

The exact token is checked in the =authorize-pickups= phase of
=e-board--advance-input-finalization= at
=lisp/core/e-board.el:3427-3484=.  Once authorized,
=e-board--classification-group-subscription= at lines 3323-3346 stores only
the participant id and subscription ids.  The prepare/commit functions at
lines 3348-3424 neither retain nor revalidate the subscription token or exact
participant lifetime.  =e-board-retire-subscription-exact= at lines 2558-2615
cancels timers, prepared effects, replay, and the id-table entry, but cannot
remove this already-grouped bucket.

When the stale classifier commits after completed retirement,
=e-board-runtime--producer-routing-finished= at
=lisp/core/e-board-runtime.el:664-688= resolves whichever attachment is current
for the participant id.  With no replacement, the revoked-delivery branch at
lines 3048-3072 fails the pickup but has no exact attachment through which to
terminally settle the producer record.  With a replacement, the new attachment
is discovered and receives the old input.

*** Evidence

With =e-board-input-classification-drain-limit= bound to 1, the probe advanced
an input through exact route authorization and grouping, stopped at
=prepare-pickups=, then retired the attachment/participant/subscription.

Without a replacement, completing the classifier yielded:

#+begin_example
message routing state: routed
pickup state:          failed
producer items:        1
settlement callback:   not called
delivery record:       retained
#+end_example

After admitting a same-id replacement before completing the same grouped
classifier, the replacement delivery function ran once and the producer
delivery record named the replacement attachment.  The committed route test at
=test/e-board-test.el:1820-1943= retires before invoking the classifier drain,
so its token check occurs before grouping and does not cover this phase.

*** Why it matters

This crosses both authorization and causal lifetime boundaries: work admitted
under a retired route is routed to a replacement that never authorized or
matched it.  The no-replacement variant also leaks trusted producer accounting
and can block quiescence.  Board-monotonic allocation is not sufficient if the
token is discarded before the final routing transaction.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Carry exact subscription token/object and participant lifetime through grouped
classifier buckets and revalidate them immediately before preparing/committing
the atomic pickup set, or make exact route retirement remove/fence every grouped
entry without scanning unrelated classifiers.  Preserve the bounded fanout and
atomic routing transaction.  Independently ensure a revoked/stale pickup can
terminally settle its producer record even when no current attachment exists.
Add phase-controlled tests that retire after each authorize/group/prepare step,
with no replacement and with same-id participant/subscription/attachment
replacement, and verify routing, producer count, FIFO state, and callback
idempotence.

After correcting both findings, update =docs/architecture.md=,
=plan.org=, and =round-5-audit.org=: their present complete-fencing,
pickup-generation, and retry-safe invocation claims overstate the verified
behavior.

* Architecture review

** Ownership, dependencies, and side effects

The physical owners are appropriate.  Durable session/board association and
journal values stay with session/board owners.  Chat service owns bindings,
clients, cursors, presentation subscriptions, and service timers.  Runtime owns
attachments, endpoint generation, producer/FIFO adaptation, activity,
invocation, reconciliation, and harness-event translation.  Registry owns live
board/client/participant catalogs.  Board owns classification, subscription
tokens/timers/effects/replay, pickup state, and routing publication.

The require graph is acyclic and the new terminal calls point downward through
public contracts.  No service reaches runtime-private maps; no registry/board
edge points upward into runtime/service.  Provider, process, storage, and
display effects remain in adapters/application shells.  The defects are
transition-order/lifetime gaps inside existing owners, not evidence for another
generic coordinator or speculative abstraction.

** Mutable state, compatibility, and substitutability

The new exact tokens, indexes, generations, and retirement stages use the
smallest correct process lifetime in intent.  Attachment producer/activity
indexes and the board-monotonic token are reconstructible and appropriately
non-durable.  The invocation count/table transition and pickup scheduled bit
currently end at different moments; the grouped classifier discards the token
too early.  Those mismatched lifetimes are the two findings.

Exact client detach and the public board route operation are real, narrow
interfaces.  Exact attachment and participant retirement are real boundaries,
but the complete terminal contract is not yet substitutable under notification
fault, reentrancy, retained pickup callback, or mid-classifier replacement.
Strengthen these postconditions without fallback, error swallowing, service
locator, broad callback registry, or private compatibility alias.

No durable/wire compatibility change is required.  Unexpected errors should
continue to surface, but owner state must already be terminal or resumable when
they do.  Obsolete id-only teardown, manual partial registry route cleanup, and
replacement-port fallback remain removed.  No additional legacy path should be
added to work around the two defects.

** Documentation accuracy

=docs/architecture.md:219-240= and the latest Round 5 audit collectively say
all exact attachment work is settled, pickup drains are generation-fenced, and
the subscription token fences classifiers universally.  The invocation,
pickup, and grouped-classifier probes contradict those claims.  Documentation
should be corrected with the implementation, not treated as evidence that the
postconditions already exist.

* Design self-check

1. *If this package stopped here, would the project be better off?*  Yes.  The
   semantic modules, exact retirement direction, producer indexes, activity
   generation, and board token are concrete improvements.  The package must
   not be accepted as complete while two lifecycle defects remain.
2. *What final direction does this move toward?*  Stable application facades
   over cohesive owners, with one exact, retry-safe lifetime from presentation
   binding through runtime attachment, registry membership, and board route.
3. *Which decisions are stable, uncertain, or likely to change?*  Durable
   association, exact replacement fencing, downward ownership, error
   visibility, and terminal settlement are stable.  Provider/storage/canvas
   policy remains local.  Invocation transition order and drain/token
   representation are owner-local implementation decisions, not reasons for a
   speculative framework.
4. *What component owns this behavior, and why?*  Runtime owns attachment,
   invocation, pickup scheduling, producer, activity, and reconciliation
   because they share endpoint generation and settlement.  Board owns route
   classification/token/effects because they share the atomic routing
   transaction.  The findings stay in those owners.
5. *Does this reduce coupling, and are interfaces narrow?*  Static coupling is
   reduced and interfaces are narrow.  Hidden temporal coupling remains where
   invocation count/table, pickup queue/scheduler, and classifier/token state
   stop sharing one terminal lifetime.
6. *Did a dependency start pointing the wrong way?*  No.  The 709-edge graph
   is acyclic, calls point service -> runtime -> registry -> board, and no new
   reverse/private service edge was introduced.
7. *Could any side effect move outward?*  External I/O/display/provider effects
   are already at edges.  Invocation settlement, pickup scheduling, and route
   commit are domain/runtime transitions and belong in their current owners;
   moving them into chat or transport would weaken ownership.
8. *What state is added or mutated, who owns it, and how long should it live?*
   Tokens, producer/turn/pickup/activity indexes, generations, counters, and
   retirement stages are reconstructible process state.  Their correct life is
   through the last captured callback/owned request.  The pickup bit and
   grouped route token currently end too late/early respectively, and the
   invocation count can end twice.
9. *What is the performance impact?*  Ordinary paths remain O(1), indexed, or
   fixed-page bounded.  Retirement is proportional to exact owned work; its
   invocation scan is terminal only.  The corrective work can remain O(1) per
   invocation/drain and bounded per classifier page.  No unrelated global scan
   or durable rewrite is justified.
10. *Are abstractions real and implementations substitutable?*  The public
    exact operations represent real active/closing/closed and replacement
    variation.  They are not fully substitutable for their documented
    postconditions until notification/reentrancy, old pickup callbacks, and
    mid-classifier replacement behave identically to ordinary teardown.
11. *What compatibility expectations apply?*  Preserve public commands,
    durable event/session formats, pending retained messages, association,
    provider semantics, and explicit errors.  Core struct changes require a
    restart.  No private shim, id fallback, or weakened precondition applies.
12. *What legacy code can be removed now?*  Previously removed id-only/manual
    teardown and private bridges stay absent.  No new legacy code is required;
    the correction can replace the pickup boolean with exact authority and
    remove any duplicate invocation terminal decrement.
13. *Where are expected errors handled, and where do unexpected errors
    surface?*  Stale/detached/revoked/closed/replaced states are handled at
    owner boundaries.  Unexpected callback/lower-owner errors surface.  Most
    remediation paths now preserve retry state, but invocation notification
    faults do not, which is an open finding.
14. *What tests prove core behavior independently?*  Direct owner suites,
    public composition, restart/storage tests, exact attached-turn tests,
    service lifecycle matrix, graph/private/duplicate checks, isolated
    graphical evidence, full ERT, and ten independent phase/failure probes form
    the evidence.  Missing committed regressions are exactly the invocation
    fault/reentrancy, pickup old/new scheduler, and classifier-after-grouping
    cases identified above; their fixes should change only runtime/board owner
    tests.
