** TODO Medium: Runtime attachment retirement cannot finish after partial owner failure

*** Problem

=e-board-runtime-retire-attachment= documents an idempotent exact terminal
operation.  It removes the exact attachment/session/endpoint maps at
=lisp/core/e-board-runtime.el:962-967= before calling
=e-board-registry-retire-participant-exact= at lines 968-969.  If that lower
owner signals after map removal, a retry no longer satisfies =owns-runtime-p=
at lines 937-944 and therefore skips the participant operation permanently.

*** Evidence

The injected first-call failure and public retry left all three runtime maps
empty but both participant catalogs and the ordinary active/indexed route live;
the registry operation was called only once.  This is an actual partial state
supported by the public operation's order, not a same-id replacement that
should be preserved.

The committed idempotence test repeats only a fully successful call.  It cannot
exercise the intermediate state and therefore remains green.

*** Why it matters

Service retirement can surface an unexpected lower-owner error after it has
already fenced/detached presentation state.  Its later retry then completes
service catalog removal while leaving an orphan participant and ordinary route
behind.  The public operation is not substitutable for its documented
idempotent complete-retirement contract, and subsequent re-ensure can inherit
or conflict with stale process state.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Make the terminal owner transition retryable without weakening exact replacement
protection.  Retire the exact participant/routes before relinquishing the map
triple, or retain an exact retirement token/tombstone that authorizes only this
attachment to finish the lower-owner step after maps are removed.  Do not fall
back to id-only participant deletion.  Add a direct test that injects failure at
each owner boundary, repeats retirement, and proves all exact catalogs/routes
are gone while a different replacement object remains untouched.

** TODO Medium: Retirement strands producer work and admits late activity for a dormant attachment

*** Problem

The runtime operation retires current FIFO entries, pending pickup cells,
existing activity mailboxes, and invocations at
=lisp/core/e-board-runtime.el:957-961=.  It never settles matching entries in
=e-board-runtime--producer-deliveries= or
=e-board-runtime--producer-turns=.  For pending/ready/in-flight FIFO states,
=e-board-runtime--retire-pickups-for-attachment= changes the board pickup but
does not invoke the producer settlement operations at lines 495-545.  For an
already consumed pickup, the only normal terminal path is
=e-board-runtime--settle-producer-turn= at lines 1595-1607, reached from a
harness terminal event inside the exact-current-attachment guard at lines
1611-1636.  Once retirement removes the maps, the old completion is correctly
fenced from publication but cannot decrement the producer unsettled count.

Separately, board enrollment installs a Work activity closure at lines
1256-1259.  =e-board-runtime--capture-work-activity= at lines 1194-1214 does not
check current attachment identity or lifecycle state.  Retirement removes
mailboxes that exist at that moment but does not detach or fence this handle
observer, so later progress can recreate a mailbox and pending queue entry for
a dormant attachment.  Because the global mailbox and pending-set keys are only
the Work id, an old same-id callback can also replace or suppress a new
attachment's mailbox before the drain rejects the stale attachment.

*** Evidence

The consumed-producer probe remains =dispatched= with one producer-turn entry,
one producer-delivery entry, unsettled count 1, and no settlement both after
retirement and after the retained old =turn-finished=.  The activity probe
shows zero mailboxes immediately after retirement and one mailbox/pending entry
again after late progress.  A later drain suppresses the stale publication, but
a hostile or stopped scheduler leaves the stale resource indefinitely, keeps
the global scheduled bit from requesting a replacement drain, and ongoing
progress can repeat it.

The committed runtime test exercises ready/accepted FIFO and current mailbox /
invocation cleanup, but its pickups are not tied to trusted producer accounting;
it does not cover producer-backed FIFO states, the consumed producer-turn
handoff, or an active Work callback retained after retirement.

*** Why it matters

A terminal binding can leave runtime quiescence permanently blocked by an
unsettled trusted producer request.  It can also accumulate process-local work
for an attachment that no longer owns any maps or participant route, or collide
with a new same-id Work mailbox.  Allowing the old harness event through the
general current guard would be unsafe because it could publish into a
replacement lifetime; the retirement owner must instead settle its exact causal
work explicitly.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Record exact attachment identity with producer delivery/turn ownership and,
during retirement, terminally settle only producer work owned by that
attachment before clearing its FIFO and turn-delivery index.  Attachment-local
pickup and turn ids provide bounded starting sets; avoid a board-global scan and
protect a replacement attachment with exact identity/generation.  Gate the
installed Work activity capture closure on the exact current active attachment
(or add an exact Work-owner observer detach seam), while preserving the
existing harness-event guard, so retained callbacks are no-ops before mailbox
mutation.  When
attachment cleanup empties a runtime queue, reconcile its scheduled state or
fence the retained drain by generation so new work can schedule independently.
Add tests for producer-backed pending/ready/accepted/consumed states, consumed
turn completion before/after retirement, quiescence counters, late progress
under retained scheduler callbacks, and a concurrent replacement attachment.

** TODO Medium: Exact participant retirement does not fence deferred subscription work from a replacement lifetime

*** Problem

=e-board-registry-retire-participant-exact= at
=lisp/core/e-board-registry.el:1104-1169= exact-checks the registry/source
participant objects, then manually marks each ordinary subscription cancelled,
cancels known timer handles, clears its accumulator, increments its two local
generations, and removes its id-table entry.  It does not execute the board
owner's prepared-activation fence at
=lisp/core/e-board.el:2459-2467=, remove queued classifier/replay records, or give
timer callbacks an identity that cannot be reused by a later subscription
object.

Prepared post-input effects capture the old subscription/activation and commit
whenever the activation remains =prepared= at lines 2785-2846.  Quiet and
lifetime callbacks resolve the current subscription by id plus an
object-local generation at lines 2661-2670 and 2690-2764.  A new same-id
subscription starts its counters again and can equal a retained old callback's
generation.  A frozen input classifier resolves current state only by
subscription id at lines 2410-2418 and 3262-3315.  Retained replay snapshots
similarly use the subscription id when they re-enter current classification at
lines 2629-2659 and 2715-2724.

*** Evidence

After exact retirement and same-id participant admission, a prepared old
activation changed from =prepared= to =committed= and produced a pickup for the
replacement participant.  In the hostile quiet-timer probe, both old and new
subscriptions reached generation 1; invoking the old callback cleared the new
accumulator, incremented its firing count, and scheduled an effect.

Known timer cancellation is insufficient because a scheduler can retain and
invoke its callback, and the public contract expressly claims replacement
fencing.  The same identity defect applies to queued input classifiers, expiry
callbacks, and retained subscription replay when ids/generations are reused.

*** Why it matters

An operation named =retire-participant-exact= can allow the retired
participant's already-prepared policy to publish under a newly admitted
participant with the same durable id.  That crosses authorization and causal
lifetime boundaries: old work affects a replacement that did not prepare it.
It also violates the architecture/audit claim that all ordinary routes and
queued callbacks are fenced.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Give the board owner one exact ordinary-route retirement operation and have the
registry call it, rather than duplicating only part of the board subscription
transition.  It must cancel the exact subscription's queued classifiers,
prepared activations, and replays and make readiness/lifetime/effect callbacks
require exact subscription object or a board-monotonic lifetime token, not a
reusable id/local counter.
Preserve immutable historical snapshots needed for event history, but prevent
them from applying new effects.  Add same-id participant/subscription tests for
queued classification, prepared activation, retained quiet and expiry timers,
queued effects, replay, partial/repeated retirement, and a replacement that must
remain unchanged.

After correcting implementation, update =docs/architecture.md=,
=plan.org=, and =round-5-audit.org= so their idempotence and complete-callback-
fencing claims match the verified semantics.

* Architecture review

** Ownership, state, and side effects

The physical owners remain appropriate.  Durable session/board association and
journal facts stay in session/board owners.  Chat service owns presentation
bindings, clients, observers, cursors, and service timer generations.  Board
runtime owns attachment admission/maps, participant FIFO adaptation,
producer/activity/invocation/reconciliation state, and harness-event
translation.  Registry owns concrete board/client/participant catalogs.  Board
owns subscription classification, timers, prepared effects, replay, pickup, and
publication.  Provider/process/storage/display side effects remain at adapters
or shells.

The defects occur where implementation stops short of those declared owners:
runtime gives up the identity needed to retry the registry side effect, omits
producer state from attachment retirement, and permits a retained Work callback
to recreate activity state.  Registry manually mutates board subscription
fields without invoking the board owner's full deferred-work fence.  No new
generic owner is warranted; each fix belongs in the existing owner boundary.

** Dependency direction and interfaces

The 709-edge require graph is acyclic.  The remediation's call direction points
from unstable application composition toward stable runtime/registry/board
contracts.  No production source reaches upward into =e-chat-service= or from
chat service into runtime-private maps.  Exact client teardown is narrow and
substitutable.  Exact attachment and participant teardown are semantically real
interfaces but currently violate their documented postconditions under partial
failure and deferred work, so they are not yet substitutable terminal ports.

The correction should strengthen these contracts rather than add a fallback,
service locator, callback registry, broad defensive swallowing, or private
compatibility shim.  Board-owned exact route retirement is a concrete variation
point already demanded by active/closing/closed teardown and same-id reuse; it
is not speculative abstraction.

** Mutable state and lifetime

No new durable state is required.  Exact attachment/client/subscription
identity is process-local and must live until every callback or owned request
that can mutate state is fenced or terminally settled.  A boolean scheduled
flag, an id with a resettable object-local generation, or maps deleted before
lower cleanup finishes are too short-lived to prove that terminal condition.

Repairable main observer state correctly retains the existing binding,
attachment, durable board association, and pending cursor.  Terminal cleanup
must retain durable identity while deleting all reconstructible process state.
The failed probes show producer turn/delivery state, late mailboxes, ordinary
prepared effects, and retained callbacks are still process-live after the
claimed owner lifetime ended.

** Compatibility, errors, and documentation

No public command, JSONL/index/checkpoint format, board journal envelope,
provider request shape, or external wire contract changed.  Same-id durable
participant/session identity is precisely why process generations must fence
old callbacks.  Obsolete private paths remain removed; no compatibility alias
is needed.

Expected stale/replaced/closed conditions are handled at owner boundaries and
normal terminal calls are idempotent.  Unexpected lower-owner errors correctly
surface, but the current mutation order makes a surfaced error non-recoverable
by the same public operation.  Error visibility and retry-safe state must both
hold.

The updated architecture and audit are not yet accurate enough for acceptance.
=docs/architecture.md:218-225= says attachment retirement settles all owned
work and routes; =round-5-audit.org:646-659= and 736-744 claim complete
idempotent callback/route postconditions; =plan.org:313-320= claims every
process-local catalog and ordinary subscription is removed.  The direct probes
contradict those statements.  Documentation should be corrected with the code,
not treated as evidence that the behavior exists.

** Legacy removal

The remediation correctly removes id-only teardown from owner paths, nil-valued
service catalog entries, and unfenced chat-service callback behavior.  The next
correction can remove the registry's partial manual imitation of board
subscription retirement and replace reusable id/local-generation callback
authorization with one exact board-owned lifetime.  Historical event/replay
values needed for durable causal explanation may remain immutable, but their
ability to execute after terminal retirement must be removed.

* Design self-check

1. *If this work package stopped here, would the project be better off than if
   it had not been built?*  Yes, the terminal ownership direction, exact client
   lease, service callback fencing, and normal recovery are improvements.  It
   must not be accepted as complete because the three remaining defects leak or
   misapply runtime work.
2. *What final direction does this move toward?*  Stable application facades
   over bounded semantic owners, with one exact and retry-safe terminal lifetime
   from presentation binding through runtime attachment and board routes.
3. *Which decisions are likely to change, uncertain, or stable?*  Durable board
   association, exact replacement fencing, owner direction, and terminal
   settlement are stable.  Provider, SQLite, and future canvas policy remain
   local.  Retry tokens and exact subscription lifetime representation are
   implementation choices that should remain owner-local rather than becoming
   speculative frameworks.
4. *What component owns this behavior, and why?*  Chat service owns presentation
   orchestration; runtime owns attachment/producer/activity state; registry owns
   catalog identity; board owns subscriptions/effects/replay.  Each finding is
   inside the owner with the causal state needed to decide it.
5. *Does this change increase or reduce coupling?*  Static coupling is reduced
   and points downward.  Hidden temporal coupling remains because map removal,
   producer settlement, Work callbacks, and board subscription work do not share
   a complete lifetime fence.  Narrow exact owner operations reduce it.
6. *Did any dependency start pointing the wrong way?*  No new production load
   or call edge points upward; the graph is acyclic.  Registry does, however,
   duplicate an incomplete board-internal transition.  Delegate the full exact
   route operation downward rather than reaching around the board owner.
7. *Could any side effect be moved outward?*  I/O, display, process, and
   provider effects are already at edges.  Participant route mutation, producer
   settlement, mailbox capture, and effect cancellation are domain/runtime side
   effects and belong in their current owners, not a UI shell.
8. *What state does this change add or mutate?*  It mutates reconstructible
   binding/client/attachment/participant/subscription/timer/mailbox/producer
   state.  Durable association correctly survives.  Exact process identity must
   outlive all captured callbacks; current runtime maps and resettable
   subscription generations do not.
9. *What is the performance impact?*  Healthy drains and normal submit remain
   bounded and green.  Complete retirement necessarily performs work
   proportional to the exact attachment's turns/mailboxes/routes.  Use
   attachment-owned keys or indexes; no unrelated global scan or durable rewrite
   is justified.
10. *Are abstractions and interfaces semantically real and substitutable?*
    Exact client detach is real and substitutable.  Exact attachment and
    participant retirement are real needs, but partial failure, producer leaks,
    and same-id deferred callbacks violate their advertised postconditions.
11. *What compatibility expectations apply?*  Preserve public commands, event
    and durable formats, pending retained messages, board association, and
    provider semantics.  Remove obsolete process state.  Do not add replacement
    fallback, alias, or unsupported-operation behavior.
12. *What legacy code can be removed now?*  Remove the registry's incomplete
    manual subscription teardown once a board-owned exact operation exists, and
    remove reusable id-only callback authorization for terminal subscription
    work.  Previously removed private bridges and duplicate policy stay absent.
13. *Where are expected errors handled, and where do unexpected errors surface?*
    Stale receiver, detach, unsubscribe, replacement, and close are expected and
    handled before chat progress.  Unexpected lower-owner retirement errors
    surface, but retry currently skips remaining cleanup; preserve visibility
    while making the public operation resumable.
14. *What tests prove core behavior independently of the full system?*  Owner
    suites, public composition, restart/storage, exact attached-turn regression,
    service lifecycle matrix, graph/private/duplicate checks, graphical
    evidence, and 2,572-case ERT are broad.  Missing direct tests must cover
    lower-owner failure/retry, consumed producer settlement and quiescence, late
    Work callbacks, and same-id prepared/timer/expiry/replay work; those tests
    should fail narrowly in runtime or board/registry owners.
