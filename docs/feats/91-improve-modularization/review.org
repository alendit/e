** TODO Medium: The extracted runtime-admission owner has no independent error contract and runtime imports are transitive

*** Expected

=F91-OWNER=, =F91-RUNTIME=, and the architecture dependency rule require a
physical owner to load and behave through its declared lower contracts.  Its
typed failures must be ordinary =error= conditions, and callers must declare
the contracts they call instead of relying on another module's imports.

*** Observed

=lisp/core/e-board-runtime-admission.el:20-21= requires only =cl-lib= and
=e-board-admission=, but it signals =e-board-runtime-error= at lines 110, 113,
119, 133, 148, 153, 169, 173, 194, 198, 207, 263, 404, 487, and 492.  That
condition is defined only in the upward facade at
=lisp/core/e-board-runtime.el:27=.  The direct owner test at
=test/e-board-runtime-admission-owner-test.el:68-81= verifies only that the
source lacks an upward =require=; its normal-path tests never trigger a typed
owner failure.

A fresh =emacs --batch -Q -L lisp/core= load reports:

#+begin_example
features owner=t runtime=nil
runtime-error-conditions=nil
peculiar error: "Board admission is still in flight" ...
#+end_example

The intended =e-board-runtime-error= and generic =error= handlers do not catch
it.  Therefore the direct owner is loadable but not behaviorally substitutable.

The facade also calls =e-board-admission-complete-p= at
=e-board-runtime.el:1962, 2022, and 2042= without importing
=e-board-admission=, and its 58 public Board calls rely on
=e-board-registry='s transitive =e-board= import.  Accordingly, the literal
166/715 graph omits semantic runtime->Board and runtime->admission edges even
though the architecture diagram names them.

*** Why it matters

A direct owner consumer receives an untyped top-level failure instead of the
advertised runtime condition.  A harmless refactor of registry or admission
imports can also break fresh runtime loading.  This defeats the extraction's
independent contract and makes the documented dependency graph misleading.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Place the base runtime error condition in a lower contract loaded before the
owner signals it (the owner itself may own that base), and have the facade's
specialized errors extend that one definition.  Declare =e-board= directly in
runtime.  Either declare =e-board-admission= for its direct predicate call or
expose that predicate through the runtime-admission owner's narrow public
contract.  Do not introduce an upward owner-to-facade edge.

*** Required evidence

Add a bare fresh-process test that triggers and catches an owner failure as
=e-board-runtime-error= and =error= while =e-board-runtime= remains unloaded;
add a fresh facade operation; rerun the literal and semantic graphs and direct
owner/runtime suites.

** TODO Medium: Compact event and index completion can forget unfinished neighbour repair

*** Expected

=F91-OWNER= and =F91-PRESERVATION= require committed-node finalization to be
retryable after every mutation boundary.  Publishing the compact node must not
clear the receipt until every exact neighbour points to the compact lifetime.

*** Observed

=e-board-admission--finalize-event-node= at
=lisp/core/e-board-admission.el:458-520= and the index analogue at lines
523-581 first replace the node-index entry, then retarget previous/next links
only inside a guard that requires the map still to contain the original full
receipt.  If a neighbour setter signals after the compact map value is already
authoritative, the full receipt remains for the first retry.  On retry the
guard is false because the map contains the compact node, so lines 508-519 or
567-579 clear the old receipt without repairing the neighbour.

Independent event and index reproductions inject the fault after compact-map
publication and before neighbour mutation.  After repeated completion, the
next compact node still has =previous-node= equal to the now-cleared full
receipt, not the published compact node.  Both
=e-f91-3cf56-board-compaction-neighbour-fault-is-not-repaired= and
=e-f91-3cf56-index-compaction-neighbour-fault-is-not-repaired= reproduce it.

*** Why it matters

The retained topology is the exact authority for later append, unlink, and
rollback.  A stale cleared receipt in that topology can make a later exact
operation skip or mutate the wrong neighbour and contradicts the claimed
resumability of the compact representation.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Retain the exact published compact node and a finalization stage in the receipt.
On retry, accept either original-receipt or exact-compact-node map authority,
repair every captured neighbour postcondition, and clear the receipt only after
all links acknowledge that compact node.  Keep this event/index-specific; no
generic transaction abstraction is needed.

*** Required evidence

Add direct owner tests for previous and next neighbour failure before/after
mutation for event and index nodes, including repeated completion, middle/tail
nodes, and a later append/unlink through the repaired topology.

** TODO Medium: Classifier, deadline, and effect drains can consume retry authority before semantic completion

*** Expected

=F91-OWNER= and =F91-PRESERVATION= require one exact FIFO item to remain
discoverable until its count, node, semantic event/effect, and successor
scheduler boundaries are acknowledged.  A callback failure may surface, but it
must not strand prepared state or a future queue.

*** Observed

Three related public-drain failures remain:

1. =e-board-drain-terminal-classifications= at
   =e-board.el:1240-1334= treats a prepared invocation as if retry settlement
   succeeded.  =e-board--settle-invocation= changes =open= to =prepared= before
   its activation event at lines 1336-1356.  If event append fails before
   mutation, the classifier retains the receipt once; on retry the non-open
   settlement is a no-op, the object is popped, and the classifier receipt is
   removed with no queued effect.  The invocation remains permanently
   =prepared=.
2. Classifier and deadline inverse operations unlink and decrement count before
   node removal at =e-board-admission.el:1515-1695= and 1906-2081.  A node
   =remhash= fault before or after mutation leaves =removed-p= false after the
   FIFO/count authority is gone.  Classifier may already have prepared its
   semantic object; deadline has not yet called settlement.  Both fault modes
   reproduce an empty queue/count zero with no viable semantic retry.  Deadline
   settlement itself also removes its receipt before
   =e-board--settle-aggregation= at =e-board.el:1186-1234=; an activation-event
   fault leaves a prepared aggregation and no effect or deadline.
3. =e-board-admission-drain-effects= catches a failing effect, but its
   =effect-drain-failed= event append at
   =e-board-admission.el:2564-2571= is outside a handler.  If that diagnostic
   event fails, control exits before lines 2578-2582 clear/reschedule the
   callback state.  The consumed queue is empty and count is zero while
   =effects-scheduled= remains true; a later healthy effect is queued without a
   callback and never runs.

The corresponding five defect witnesses pass.  Positive controls also prove
that pre-count restoration and a two-item post-count classifier successor work,
so the finding is limited to node/semantic/failure-log boundaries rather than
the whole drain design.

*** Why it matters

These paths convert visible failures into permanently prepared invocations or
aggregations, lost timeouts/classifications, and queued effects with no
scheduler authority.  Retry and quiescence can then report a state that cannot
make progress.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Give classifier/deadline semantic terminalization an explicit exact stage; do
not treat merely =prepared= as successful completion.  Preserve or reconstruct
the same receipt's queue/node authority until both semantic and node stages are
acknowledged.  Ensure an effect callback or diagnostic-event failure reaches a
single scheduling-finalization path that clears the consumed callback and
publishes exactly one successor while preserving the first error.

*** Required evidence

Add direct public-drain tests for classifier/deadline node removal before and
after mutation, activation-event failure before and after mutation, repeated
retry, multi-item FIFO ordering, effect callback plus diagnostic-event failure,
old/new callback order, scheduled/generation bits, and quiescence counts.

** TODO Medium: Runtime catalog completion can lose or borrow exact authority

*** Expected

=F91-RUNTIME= requires one exact runtime admission record to remain reachable
through a supplied Board/attachment scope until Board completion and all six
catalog inverses are acknowledged.  Cleanup may remove only bucket objects
captured by that record, must preserve replacement authority, and must treat a
postmutation final removal as the proven successful postcondition.

*** Observed

=e-board-runtime-admission-complete= at
=lisp/core/e-board-runtime-admission.el:312-407= has three holes:

1. Final recovery-primary =remhash= at lines 393-397 immediately re-signals an
   adapter error even when the record is already absent.  All six catalog
   memberships are then gone, so neither the caller nor a later retry can find
   the exact record.  The direct witness observes six zeros plus a surfaced
   error.  Through public enrollment, Board Work and invocation relations are
   already committed while the runtime invocation lease/count are dropped;
   retry is a no-op and reenrollment rejects the duplicate relation.
2. Lines 321-337 choose each cleanup bucket as =record-bucket OR current outer
   bucket=.  If publication stopped before the record ever acquired a later
   bucket and a replacement lifetime publishes an empty bucket at that key,
   old completion adopts and deletes that unrecorded replacement.  Exact
   identity fencing is therefore bypassed precisely for a missing record slot.
3. =remember= publishes the recovery primary before its Board/attachment
   indexes.  A first fault before the recovery-Board outer publication and a
   later pre-mutation fault removing the recovery primary leave the record only
   in the global recovery table.  Public =retry attachment board= scans the four
   object-local secondary buckets and cannot discover it, so the catalog entry
   remains permanently retained.

The ten-stage publication matrix passes before/after retry.  Removal stages one
through nine pass before/after and final removal passes before mutation.  The
three failures above are therefore exact residual boundaries, not a rejection
of the six-index design.  The same-Board sibling-bucket union is documented and
did not independently demonstrate harmful public behavior, so it is not a
separate finding.

*** Why it matters

The defects can expose failure after semantic success, leave an unreachable
runtime lease, delete a replacement lifetime's scope, or retain a global
record forever.  Later retire, re-ensure, or submit cannot reliably repair those
states through the public object-local contract.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Use only bucket identities stored in the record; absence means no authority to
remove a current outer bucket.  Treat final recovery removal as successful when
the exact record is proven absent after a signalled primitive.  Before a caller
can lose its exact token, ensure any retained recovery-primary record also has
at least one exact object-local index, or retain a narrow exact retry handle.
Do not add a process-wide scan.  Preserve the initiating error unless cleanup
itself remains incomplete.

*** Required evidence

Extend the direct owner and public enrollment suites with final =remhash=
before/after cases, missing-record-slot plus replacement bucket, primary-only
two-fault recovery, persistent faults, repeated completion, replacement
attachment/participant, retire/re-ensure/submit, and exact catalog/count/lease
postconditions.

** TODO Low: Compact work-index queues retain one full rollback receipt per queue

*** Expected

=F91-TOPOLOGY= and the smallest-lifetime rule require committed Board indexes to
retain only topology needed for later exact neighbour repair.  Rollback-only
transaction records should become unreachable after successful completion.

*** Observed

=e-board-index-link-node= at =e-board-admission.el:56-60= has nine fields plus
the struct tag, so its runtime vector has ten slots.  Finalization copies the
full receipt's =root-node= into each compact node at lines 535-544.  For each
singleton queue that root is the original 19-slot =e-board-index-receipt=.
Although token receipt lists and many receipt fields are cleared, the compact
node keeps that full receipt reachable.

The 10,000-queue probe observes 10,000 compact nodes and 10,000 distinct full
root receipts.  By contrast, the 10,000-event chain retains one constant full
root receipt, not one per event.  The plan and audit statements that both
representations are nine-slot nodes are also inaccurate for the index node.

*** Why it matters

Singleton work-index queues are a realistic high-cardinality shape.  Exact
repair justifies a compact root identity, but it does not justify a second full
rollback vector for every committed queue.  This is bounded per queue and has
no demonstrated latency regression, so severity is Low.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Make the compact root node self-authoritative, or retain a minimal compact root
sentinel with only the exact topology required by later append/unlink.  Do not
weaken exact identity fencing or introduce a shared generic node framework.
Correct the slot-count evidence.

*** Required evidence

Add singleton and multi-node work-index lifetime tests that count reachable
full receipts after completion, followed by append/middle-unlink/abort checks;
rerun the 10,000-queue shape and bounded timing probe.

* Architecture review

** Owner and dependency direction

The new runtime-admission module is economically justified.  Its six catalogs,
record stages, and remember/fence/retry/complete operations have one change
reason: preserve exact cross-owner Board admission authority across runtime
attachment reentry and cleanup.  Keeping them in runtime had made the runtime
facade exceed 4,000 lines; extracting them does not create a generic framework
or split attachment delivery state.  Direct 5-test discovery confirms a
substantive root.

Dependency direction is otherwise downward and acyclic:

#+begin_example
e-board-admission          -> e-board-state, e-work
e-board                    -> e-board-state, e-board-admission, e-work
e-board-runtime-admission  -> e-board-admission
e-board-runtime            -> registry, runtime-admission, harness/session/Work
#+end_example

There is no owner-to-runtime-facade reverse call.  Finding 1 is nevertheless a
real contract violation: the lower owner names an upward-defined error, while
runtime's Board/admission use is only transitively loaded.  The literal graph
is acyclic and correctly counted, but explicit dependencies must match the
semantic call graph before the architecture claim is complete.

** State, lifetime, and side effects

| State | Owner and lifetime | Review disposition |

| Event sequence, routing, publication, subscriptions, processing, activity, aggregation policy | =e-board=; one process-local Board | Retain cohesive semantic aggregate. |

| Session/board association | session aggregate/board policy; durable | Preserved across process-local cleanup; no wire change. |

Scheduler, notification, event, and dispatcher effects remain at Board/runtime
application edges.  The admission owner owns exact mutation mechanics because
it alone can decide whether a pre/postmutation failure is safe to repair.
Finding 3 does not ask to move semantic policy into the receipt owner; it asks
the two owners to preserve one explicit acknowledgement across their existing
boundary.

All new records are process-local.  No session codec, storage schema, durable
event wire format, provider anchor, or public persisted key changed.  A full
Emacs restart, not a compatibility shim, is the correct activation boundary
for the new record/module shapes.

** Interfaces, compatibility, and obsolete paths

The runtime-admission public surface is narrow: remember, finish, fence, retry,
abort, and complete accept exact objects and do not expose catalogs.  Runtime
does not reach into owner-private fields.  Finding 1 requires a real typed error
contract, not broader capabilities.  Finding 4 requires stronger exact
postconditions, not an id fallback or global scan.

Receiptless work-index insertion and descriptive-id classifier/deadline
fallbacks remain removed.  Missing exact authority fails visibly.  Exact
registry detach/participant retirement remains object/generation fenced; the
retained public id-based registry compatibility path resolves current authority
before delegating and is not used for owner teardown.  No private compatibility
shim is warranted for process-local structs.

** Performance and memory

All ordinary changed lookups are exact hash/bucket or FIFO operations; no
process-wide scan was added.  The 10,000-event and 10,000-index probes are fast
in absolute terms.  Event-prefix growth is causally required for sparse message
cursors.  Exact Board/runtime recovery requires indexes, but not unbounded
rollback representations.  Finding 5 is the only economically actionable
committed-memory issue: one full receipt per work-index queue.  The constant one
event root receipt per Board chain is not separately actionable.

* Required design self-check

1. *If this package stopped here, would the project be better off than if it
   had not been built?*  Yes.  Runtime admission now has a physical owner,
   runtime is below 4,000 lines, ordinary recovery is stronger, and committed
   state is smaller.  It is not acceptable as final while five findings remain.
2. *What final direction does this move toward?*  One semantic owner per
   mutable lifetime: lower Board state, Board-specific admission mechanics,
   semantic Board policy, runtime-specific admission catalogs, and attachment
   application composition.
3. *Which decisions are stable or uncertain?*  Exact object authority,
   append/FIFO order, and durable session association are stable.  Future
   storage/provider policy remains local to existing adapters.  The candidate
   adds no speculative extension point; fixes should be owner-specific stages.
4. *Who owns the behavior and why?*  Board admission owns receipt mutation;
   Board owns routing/settlement policy; runtime admission owns the six
   cross-owner catalogs; runtime owns attachment delivery/generation.  Each has
   one primary change reason.  Findings expose incomplete handoffs, not a need
   for a generic transaction owner.
5. *Does coupling increase or decrease?*  Extraction reduces runtime/catalog
   coupling and exposes six narrow operations.  Finding 1 leaves hidden
   transitive coupling; correcting explicit imports/error ownership reduces it.
6. *Did a dependency point the wrong way?*  No require/call cycle was added,
   but the lower owner relies on an error defined only upward and runtime relies
   on transitive Board/admission loading.  Finding 1 must close both gaps.
7. *Could side effects move outward?*  Scheduler, event, dispatcher, Work, and
   session effects are already at Board/runtime edges.  Receipt postcondition
   checks belong in the lower mutation owner and cannot move outward safely.
8. *What state is added or mutated?*  Six process-local runtime catalogs and
   compact Board link nodes.  Catalogs should live only through cleanup; link
   nodes through Board/index lifetime.  Findings 2, 4, and 5 identify state
   that is cleared too early, becomes unreachable, or lives too long.
9. *What is the performance impact?*  Ordinary paths remain constant-time and
   bounded-page drains remain bounded.  10,000-item timings are small.  No
   justified CPU regression was found; the per-queue full receipt is avoidable
   memory and must not be accepted merely because the current structure is
   convenient.
10. *Are abstractions semantically real and substitutable?*  Board state,
    Board admission, and runtime admission are real.  Runtime admission is not
    yet fresh-load substitutable because its typed error is undefined, and
    drain/catalog operations weaken retry behavior at the reported boundaries.
11. *What compatibility expectations apply?*  Public Board/runtime/session
    behavior and durable formats must remain.  Process-local struct changes may
    require restart.  The established public registry id path remains; owner
    cleanup stays exact-object based.
12. *What legacy code can be removed now?*  The old in-runtime admission
    catalogs, receiptless work-index insertion, id-only classifier/deadline
    recovery, inverse scans, and full committed event-map receipts are removed.
    No fallback should be restored to fix these findings.
13. *Where are errors handled and surfaced?*  Expected stale/replaced authority
    errors surface at Board/runtime boundaries and initiating errors normally
    retain precedence.  Finding 1 produces an invalid untyped condition;
    Findings 2-4 surface errors while losing retry authority.  Those are the
    precise remaining error-contract violations.
14. *What tests prove the core independently?*  Direct 32-test Board-admission,
    5-test runtime-admission-owner, 77-test Board, 22-test runtime-admission,
    79-test runtime, and 11-test composition roots prove mechanisms and public
    composition separately.  The 22 independent probes identify the missing
    boundary tests.  Historical names and independent discovery remain intact.
