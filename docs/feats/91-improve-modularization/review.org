* Findings

** TODO Medium: Already-ready aggregation effects escape their exact admission

Expected: =F91-OWNER= and =F91-PRESERVATION= require an already-ready
aggregation's activation and deferred effect to be owned by the same supplied
admission.  If a count callback reenters and fences that admission, the outer
operation must inverse the activation, effect cell, count, and scheduling
authority together.

Observed: in =lisp/core/e-board.el:1350=,
=e-board--settle-aggregation= calls =e-board-admission-schedule-effect= at line
1380.  Reading the function form confirms that the =admission= expression at
line 1409 is the final body form of the lambda; the scheduler call has only the
=board= and =effect= arguments.  The lower wrapper therefore creates and
finalizes a separate standalone token.  The probe triggers reentry from the
effect =+1= unsettled notification.  The outer aggregation correctly errors
and disappears, but its effect remains queued with =unsettled-effect-count= 1,
=effects-scheduled=t=, and Board pending-admission count zero.

Why it matters: the caller receives failure while a ghost effect can later
invoke an invalidated target.  Board quiescence also remains nonzero.  This is
the exact aggregation-effect authority defect from the preceding review.

Smallest corrective direction: close the lambda before the third argument and
pass the supplied admission to =e-board-admission-schedule-effect=.  Add a real
ready-empty and ready-work aggregation regression that reenters from the count
and scheduler boundaries, both returning and signalling, and proves one exact
inverse with no activation/effect/count/callback survivor.

** TODO Medium: Standalone admission wrappers commit after reentrant cancellation

Expected: the new Board pending catalog makes nested related entry
cancel-requested rather than recursively inversing an active frame.  The outer
frame must postcheck that fence before it marks the operation committed or
reports success.

Observed: the standalone wrappers in
=e-board-admission.el:512-542= (event), =1101-1132= (classifier),
=1431-1458= (deadline), and =1794-1822= (effect) set =committed-p= and complete
the token immediately after their primitive returns.  None checks
=e-board-admission--cancel-requested-p= or the exact current postcondition.
Four direct probes reenter a public Board subscription from event index,
routing-count, or effect-count callbacks, catch the nested pending condition,
and let the callback return.  Every outer wrapper reports success, clears its
pending catalog entry, and leaves the event/queue/effect committed despite its
token having been fenced.

Why it matters: the in-flight fence is only useful if the owning stack honors
it.  Current code converts a rejected nested mutation into stale outer success,
and replacement/retirement callers cannot distinguish the committed ghost from
an authorized result.

Smallest corrective direction: give each standalone wrapper the same exact
post-notification current/cancel check as composed Board enrollment.  On a
fence, finish the frame, inverse the exact token, retain retry authority on
cleanup failure, and signal =e-board-admission-pending= without replacing an
earlier primary condition.  Cover return and signal reentry at every callback
boundary.

** TODO Medium: Drains can lose terminal work or strand FIFO survivors

Expected: classifier, deadline, and effect drains must make one queue cell,
node receipt, unsettled count, semantic callback, and successor scheduler an
exact staged transition.  A before/after fault must either leave the item
retryable or finish it exactly once; a surviving FIFO must always retain one
accepted fresh callback.

Observed:

- =e-board-admission-drain-effects= at
  =e-board-admission.el:1945-1997= unlinks the cell, removes its node, marks its
  receipt removed, and calls the fallible count notification before invoking
  the effect.  A notification that signals after the count mutation loses the
  effect entirely and leaves =effects-scheduled=t=.  A later healthy enqueue
  remains stranded.
- =e-board-drain-aggregation-deadlines= at =e-board.el:1186-1212= removes the
  deadline before settling its aggregation.  A post-count notification fault
  leaves the aggregation open, the queue empty, no scheduled callback, and the
  only receipt orphaned in the node index.
- =e-board-drain-terminal-classifications= at =e-board.el:1218-1273= has the
  analogous terminal receipt orphan when exact removal faults after count.
- When a bounded page leaves survivors, effect successor publication at
  =e-board-admission.el:1989-1996= sets the scheduled bit before the scheduler
  call without rollback.  Classifier and deadline successor scheduling clear
  their bits on rejection, but do not republish the already-existing survivor.
  Direct probes leave all three FIFOs nonempty with no accepted callback; the
  effect case additionally retains a falsely true scheduled bit.

Why it matters: Work aggregation deadlines may never settle, effects may be
lost or never applied, routing/quiescence may remain permanently nonzero, and
ordinary later work cannot reliably repair the queue.

Smallest corrective direction: retain the exact head receipt as drain authority
until count notification and semantic terminalization are acknowledged; make
the stages resumable rather than marking =removed= first.  Publish successor
callbacks with the same acceptance/fencing protocol used by initial enqueue,
and retain a recoverable unscheduled state when an adapter rejects.  Test all
three drains at pre/post count, callback, event, and successor-scheduler
boundaries, including old/new callback order and reentrant enqueue.

** TODO Medium: Classifier retry skips a still-pending exact index restoration

Expected: when a staged terminal classifier detached an invocation or
aggregation work-index queue, abort must restore that captured exact queue
unless a replacement is authoritative.  A restoration fault must leave the
same stage retryable.

Observed: both =e-board-admission--abort-work= at
=e-board-admission.el:2013-2046= and =--abort-aggregation= at
=2135-2169= perform index restoration inside the conditional that only runs
while the classifier receipt is not =removed=.  The first abort can completely
remove the classifier queue receipt, then fail before the captured index
=puthash=.  On retry, =removed-p= skips the entire block, cleanup is reported
complete, the Board catalog entry is deleted, and the exact invocation queue is
permanently absent.  The direct probe reproduces that exact pre-mutation
restoration fault.

Why it matters: a Work terminal relationship can disappear even though abort
claims success.  Later Work settlement no longer reaches its invocation or
aggregation.

Smallest corrective direction: stage queue removal and each detached-index
restoration independently.  Restoration must run whenever its detached flag is
true, even after the queue receipt is removed; only clear the flag after the
exact captured queue is present or a replacement is explicitly authoritative.

** TODO Medium: Board completion removes recovery authority before finalization

Expected: the comment at =e-board-admission.el:539= promises that completion
errors remain retryable through the committed catalog.  A public caller must
not receive failure after a visible non-idempotent commit unless the exact
completion token remains discoverable.

Observed: =e-board-admission-complete= at lines 268-275 sets cleanup complete,
removes the admission from =e-board-pending-admissions=, and only then drops
transient fields.  A =remhash= adapter that signals after mutation leaves the
catalog empty and the admission unfinalized.  The public standalone event probe
receives the error with the event visible, finds no pending authority, and an
ordinary retry appends a duplicate event.  The old event-node receipt still
retains its Board and event payload.

Why it matters: error visibility and committed state disagree, and recovery can
neither finish compaction nor prevent duplicate side effects.

Smallest corrective direction: keep the catalog entry until committed-state
finalization has an acknowledged postcondition, then remove the exact entry as
the final step.  If catalog removal itself signals after mutation, record enough
local stage authority for the public wrapper to prove completion rather than
reporting ambiguous failure.  Add direct public tests for both remhash orders
and repeated completion.

** TODO Medium: Runtime bucket creation leaks all four outer catalog scopes

Expected: =F91-RUNTIME= requires every runtime pending-admission record and
board/attachment secondary entry to disappear after exact abort/completion,
including faults before or after each membership mutation.  Replacement scopes
must remain untouched.

Observed: =e-board-runtime--pending-admission-use-bucket= at
=e-board-runtime.el:1152-1188= can install a new outer table bucket and then
propagate a postmutation =puthash= fault before the caller stores that bucket in
the record or adds admission membership.  Completion at lines 1380-1499 only
removes buckets reachable from recorded fields or exact admission membership.
The independently reproduced matrix covers recovery-by-board,
recovery-by-attachment, normal-by-board, and normal-by-attachment.  In every
case primary/recovery records clean up, but the selected outer table retains one
empty bucket keyed by the old Board or attachment object.

Why it matters: attachment buckets retain harness/session graphs for process
lifetime, Board buckets retain closed or replaced boards, and the catalog's
claimed exact cleanup invariant is false.

Smallest corrective direction: record bucket creation authority before the
fallible publication or have the helper return/retain an exact publication
receipt that cleanup can use even when membership was never added.  Remove only
the same empty bucket by object identity; never delete a replacement.  Keep the
four-table pre/postmutation matrix as direct runtime-owner tests.

** TODO Medium: Runtime admission recovery now has its own unextracted change reason

Expected: =F91-OWNER= and =F91-TOPOLOGY= require every retained large root to
have a causal colocation proof that addresses each material responsibility, and
source/test topology should agree.  Dependencies must be declared toward
stable contracts rather than hidden behind transitive loading.

Observed: =e-board-runtime.el= grew from 3,934 to 4,304 lines.  Lines 146-182
define six process-global pending/recovery catalogs, lines 320-334 define their
17-field transaction record, and lines 1090-1586 implement roughly 500 lines of
publication, exact membership, inverse, fencing, and recovery.  This subsystem
changes for hash/catalog transaction reasons, while attachment delivery,
producer/pickup/activity drains, reconciliation, and harness execution change
for runtime lifecycle reasons.  Tests already recognize the boundary:
=test/e-board-runtime-admission-test.el= is a substantive independent 991-line
root.

The current causal proof says splitting producer/pickup/retirement would divide
attachment settlement.  It does not explain why the pending-admission catalog

also shows runtime calling 61 Board operations and two admission-owner
operations while its literal imports declare only registry/Work/harness/session
owners; the literal 165/713 graph therefore understates the actual dependency.

Why it matters: this is the exact pattern Feature 91 is meant to remove: a
large facade/application root absorbs a second transaction owner because it
shares object types, while the test topology has already split by change
reason.  Keeping it makes the next catalog correction touch a 4,304-line
delivery adapter and leaves dependency evidence misleading.

Smallest corrective direction: extract a Board-runtime-specific private
admission/catalog owner containing the pending record, six catalogs, and
remember/finish/retry/complete operations.  Pass exact Board, attachment,
generation, and abort/complete contracts as narrow semantic values; do not
invent a generic transaction framework or move producer/pickup/retirement
state.  Declare every Board/admission dependency explicitly or route it through
the stable Board facade, and add a direct owner suite matching the existing
runtime-admission composition suite.

** TODO Low: Committed admission state is finalized logically but not physically

Expected: =F91-OWNER= and the performance rule require rollback-only state to
live only through recovery.  The audit and architecture claim committed event
nodes retain only minimum link identity and committed admissions drop transient

Observed: =e-board-admission--finalize-event-node= and
=--finalize-index-node= at =e-board-admission.el:203-241= nil fields in the
original full receipt structs but retain the structs themselves in node maps.

vector.  A 10,000-event bounded run retains 10,000 such nodes plus 10,001 prefix

does not clear the committed aggregation's =admission= slot or the admission's

nine-slot object retaining a finalized 17-slot token and the reciprocal object
graph.

Why it matters: the Board event log is a high-cardinality, Board-lifetime path.
The exact node index and prefix checkpoint are justified, but full rollback
record footprints and committed token cycles are not required by the semantic
contract.

Smallest corrective direction: replace committed event/index receipts with
small Board-specific permanent link nodes or otherwise compact their physical
shape, and clear the aggregation/token backreferences once runtime has
acknowledged completion.  Preserve only the exact neighbour identity needed by
future staged appends.  Add structural lifetime assertions, not a generic
allocator abstraction.

=e-board-admission= owns Board-specific mutation receipts and inverse stages.
=e-board= owns semantic sequence, participants, subscriptions, routing,
observers, pickup/publication, activity, processing, and settlement policy.
Neither lower owner calls upward.  There is no dependency cycle, generic
transaction framework, callback service locator, or facade reverse edge.

The new modules are therefore accepted as physical boundaries.  The remaining
=e-board.el= 4,022-line root has a causal aggregate proof after the extraction:
its remaining policies share one Board identity, event sequence, participant/
subscription map, routing decision, and settlement order.  No second extraction
from that semantic aggregate is economically required by current evidence.

=e-board-runtime.el= is different.  Attachment/delivery composition remains a
real application owner, but its pending admission catalog now has independent
state, failure stages, tests, and a primary reason to change.  Finding 7 is a
specific private owner extraction, not a generic split-by-size request.

The literal require scan remains a useful, reproducible acyclicity measure, but
the call scan must accompany it.  Runtime and registry reach Board/admission
operations through transitive imports.  Remediation should make those semantic
edges explicit so dependency documentation describes executable source, not
only load order.

** State, lifetime, side effects, and errors

All new state is process-local and rebuildable after restart; no durable
session format changed.  Exact object/generation identity is the correct
replacement fence.  Hash/list/timer state belongs to Board, Work hooks belong
to Work, attachment/catalog recovery belongs to runtime, and durable
association remains in session/registry owners.

Scheduler, count notification, Work publication, and timer functions are
correctly injected side-effect edges.  The problem is transaction order at
those edges: several drains discard state before the edge is acknowledged, and
standalone wrappers ignore the cancellation produced by reentry.  Moving the
side effect into another owner would not fix that; the current owner must keep
exact retry authority across it.

Expected validation and stale-authority errors generally surface at Board or
runtime boundaries.  Initiating errors are usually preserved.  Findings 3, 5,
and 6 show cases where the original error surfaces but cleanup authority is
lost or leaked, so the state/error contract is not yet substitutable.

** Interfaces, compatibility, and obsolete paths

The Board token/current/abort/completion APIs are narrow and consumer-shaped.
Runtime raw-field inspection is gone.  Exact registry detach/retire operations
remain object/generation-fenced.  Public id-based registry detach is retained
only for its established caller contract and resolves the current client before
delegating; owner teardown does not use it.

Receiptless work-index insertion and id-only classifier/deadline resolution are
removed.  A missing exact receipt now signals before descriptive fallback.  No
private compatibility shim is needed for process-local struct changes; a
restart is the correct boundary.

** Performance

No ordinary inverse introduces a global scan; node and catalog lookup is
constant-time, and the 10,000-event smoke is fast in absolute terms.  The
prefix map's one monotonic entry per event is justified by sparse cursor
semantics.  The full receipt vector per event and six-table runtime catalog
overhead are separate questions: exact recovery requires indexes, but it does
not require retaining rollback-only slots after commit.  Finding 8 asks for the
smallest permanent representation, not for removal of the exact indexes.

* Required design self-check

1. *If this package stopped here, would the project be better off?*  The
   extracted Board owners, exact link receipts, sparse cursor repair, and test
   topology are improvements.  The package is not acceptable as final because
   it still reports stale success, loses drain authority, and leaks catalogs.
2. *What final direction does this move toward?*  One semantic owner per
   mutable lifetime, with Board-specific exact inverse mechanics below the
   Board facade and cross-owner composition in a narrow runtime application
   service.
3. *What is stable or uncertain?*  Board/runtime ownership, public behavior,
   and durable formats are stable.  Receipt stages and catalog mechanics are
   still changing and should remain private/local.  No hypothetical generic
   transaction extension point is justified.
4. *Who owns each behavior?*  Board admission owns exact list/map/count/
   scheduler recovery; Board facade owns routing and settlement policy; runtime
   admission owns cross-owner pending catalog publication; runtime delivery
   owns attachments, producer/pickup/activity, and harness composition.
5. *Does coupling increase or decrease?*  Exact objects and lower Board modules
   reduce descriptive coupling.  The 500-line runtime catalog inside the
   delivery adapter and undeclared transitive Board calls retain avoidable
   coupling; Finding 7 removes it through a concrete narrow owner.
6. *Did a dependency point the wrong way?*  No SCC or upward lower-owner call
   exists.  The semantic call graph is underdeclared: runtime and registry call
   Board/admission operations without matching literal imports.  The fix is an
   explicit stable facade/owner dependency, not a reverse callback.
7. *Could side effects move outward?*  Schedulers, timers, Work hooks, and
   notifications are already injected edges.  Their exact acceptance must be
   staged by the current owner; moving them farther outward would split the
   same transaction.
8. *What state was added, who owns it, and how long should it live?*  Receipt
   nodes, pending catalogs, stages, generations, and prefix checkpoints are
   process-local.  Prefix/link identity may live for Board history; rollback
   fields and admission tokens should end at acknowledged completion.  Runtime
   records should end after all exact memberships are absent.
9. *What is the performance impact?*  Ordinary operations remain bounded and
   the benchmark shows no CPU concern.  Permanent 18-slot event receipts,
   committed 17-slot tokens, and empty scope buckets are avoidable memory
   regressions, not fundamental recovery costs.
10. *Are abstractions real and substitutable?*  Board state/admission owners and
    exact registry operations are real.  A runtime-specific catalog owner is
    also real.  Stale-success wrappers and drains are not yet substitutable
    because their error/retry postconditions are weaker than the facade promise.
11. *What compatibility applies?*  Public commands, event order, FIFO routing,
    durable JSONL/session/Board association, error visibility, provider
    requests, and supported registry id entrypoints remain compatible.  Private
    process structs require restart and need no shim.
12. *What legacy code can be removed now?*  Receiptless index insertion,
    descriptive classifier/deadline fallback, and raw token field access are
    removed.  Final remediation can remove committed admission backreferences
    and transitive undeclared loading assumptions.
13. *Where are errors handled?*  Validation stays at owner boundaries and
    primary conditions usually surface.  Unexpected count/scheduler/hash faults
    in Findings 2-6 currently leave stale, unreachable, or leaked state; exact
    recovery must remain reachable while the original condition is preserved.
14. *What tests prove core behavior independently?*  The 23 direct admission,
    11 Board composition, 71 Board, 79 runtime, and 22 runtime-admission tests,
    plus 21 independent probes, preservation/full ERT, fresh loads, compile,
    graph, name, private/duplicate, and bounded performance checks.  The defect
    witnesses should become focused owner regressions so later design changes
    remain local.

* Unresolved

Eight actionable findings remain:

1. already-ready aggregation effects escape their supplied admission;
2. standalone event/classifier/deadline/effect wrappers ignore reentrant
   cancellation;
3. classifier/deadline/effect drains lose terminal or successor authority;
4. classifier abort can forget exact index restoration;
5. Board completion can remove its only recovery token before finalization;
6. runtime catalog publication leaks all four empty outer bucket scopes;
7. runtime pending admission/catalog state requires a matching private physical
   owner and accurate dependency declarations; and
8. committed event/index/aggregation admission representation retains
   rollback-sized state beyond its required lifetime.

There is no deferred follow-up standing in for these defects.  Feature 91 is
not at fixed point, must remain =In-review=, and must not be marked =Done=.
