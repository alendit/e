* Findings

** TODO Medium: Runtime mistakes interrupted committed completion for rollback

*** Expected

=F91-OWNER=, =F91-RUNTIME=, and =F91-PRESERVATION= require one exact authority
and a retryable disposition at every admission stage.  Once Board enrollment
has committed and completion has begun, a map/neighbour/catalog fault must
resume committed completion.  It must not attempt the inverse for already
visible Work or an already-finalized sibling receipt.

*** Observed

=e-board-runtime.el:1944-1946= runs runtime finish/completion inside the same
condition that covers admission.  Any completion error reaches
=e-board-runtime.el:1948-1970=, which calls =finish= and then =abort=.
Object-local retry repeats that direction: =e-board-runtime-admission.el:523-530=
always tries the Board abort before runtime completion.  By contrast, the
lower Board recovery operation correctly distinguishes committed from
uncommitted tokens at =e-board-admission.el:903-906=.

The direct witness faults completion after one sibling receipt has compacted.
Two abort attempts both fail and the exact token remains pending.  Through
public enrollment, the Work projection is visible, Board pending count is one,
and =e-board-runtime-admission-retry= still errors instead of resuming
compaction.  The direct owner test at =test/e-board-admission-test.el:1131-1197=
proves retrying completion; it does not prove the composed runtime disposition.

*** Why it matters

An adapter or callback fault in a supported exact-recovery boundary turns a
successful public enrollment into a visible but permanently blocking partial
commit.  Later enrollment, retirement, or retry cannot safely infer an inverse
after some rollback authority has been finalized.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Give the runtime record an explicit semantic disposition or derive it from a
public Board admission completion stage.  Before Board commit, errors follow
the exact abort path.  Once Board completion/finalization starts, errors retain
the record and resume =e-board-admission-complete=; they never call abort.
Add composed public enrollment tests for faults after each compact sibling and
runtime-catalog completion boundary.

** TODO High: Terminal settlement can lose its only continuation and leak its admission

*** Expected

=F91-RUNTIME= and =F91-PRESERVATION= require invocation and aggregation
terminalization to be exactly-once across classifier, activation, event,
effect, cancellation, and quiescence boundaries.  External effects must not be
repeated, successful effects must not be relabeled failed, pending admissions
must eventually clear, and unexpected publication/diagnostic errors must
remain visible with an exact retry authority.

*** Observed

The semantic stages are not represented by one retryable owner:

- invocation activation changes to =prepared= before map publication at
  =e-board.el:1443-1456=.  A postmutation =puthash= signal leaves the activation
  in the map but the invocation without its activation id; the next attempt
  rejects the prepared object at =:1457-1460=;
- invocation and aggregation effects switch to =applying= and append the
  =activation-applying= event outside their inner dispatcher handlers at
  =e-board.el:1468-1480= and =:1585-1594=;
- the low-level effect drain removes the exact receipt and consumes unsettled
  count before invoking the semantic closure at
  =e-board-admission.el:2829-2869=.  A closure fault therefore has no queued
  continuation to resume;
- after an external dispatcher succeeds, an =effect-committed= append fault is
  caught as though the dispatcher failed, relabeling state =failed= at
  =e-board.el:1491-1508= and =:1605-1622=.  A failure-diagnostic append fault is
  then swallowed; and
- cancellation of a prepared aggregation at =e-board.el:1736-1760= fences the
  activation and publishes cancellation events but neither completes nor
  aborts its retained settlement admission.  The ordinary no-fault witness
  leaves Board pending count one and =e-board-admission-require-clear= errors.

=e-board--settlement-finish-admission= at =e-board.el:1415-1418= only completes
the lower token.  It does not clear the invocation/aggregation settlement
slot, activation event/effect receipt holders, or consumed effect receipt.

The independent witnesses also reproduce pre/post =activation-applying= faults
stranding state =applying=, a successful dispatcher followed by a terminal
event postfault becoming =failed=, and a missing =effect-failed= event after a
diagnostic fault.  In each case the lower effect FIFO has already consumed its
only callback authority.

*** Why it matters

This is a core semantic correctness and resource-lifetime defect.  It can
block all later Board mutation with a pending admission, lose terminal history,
or tell consumers that a successfully delivered effect failed.  Retrying the
arbitrary dispatcher would risk duplicate external side effects; doing nothing
strands state.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Represent the concrete Board settlement stages in the Board owner: activation
map publication, prepared/applying event acknowledgements, external dispatch
started/completed, terminal event acknowledgement, and admission completion.
Keep the exact semantic continuation reachable after the low-level FIFO cell
is consumed, but never re-run a dispatcher already acknowledged complete.
Cancellation must close the retained token.  Preserve the original callback
error when diagnostic publication also fails, and expose narrow public
receipt-status operations instead of reading admission-private fields.  Add
direct semantic tests for every pre/post stage, no-fault prepared cancellation,
return/signal reentry, and subsequent Board reuse.

** TODO Medium: Runtime recovery crosses attachment scope and can become object-invisible

*** Expected

Runtime recovery state belongs to one exact attachment/Board admission
lifetime.  Attachment-local retirement must affect only records captured for
that exact attachment; Board-wide recovery must be an explicit separate
operation.  Every partial publication must remain reachable through the exact
object that public retry/retire/re-ensure/submit uses, without pushing an
upper-runtime record into the lower Board token.

*** Observed

The lower Board admission structs gained a =runtime-admission-record= field at
=e-board-admission.el:326-342=.  The upper owner stores its record there before
the first catalog mutation at =e-board-runtime-admission.el:268-276= and clears
it only after all six inverses at =:432-440=.  This is an opaque exact identity,
but its ownership and lifetime flow downward from the attachment runtime into
the lower Board transaction token.

The fallback does not close the recovery contract:

- =e-board-runtime-admission-fence= combines the exact attachment buckets with
  the entire Board pending/recovery buckets and marks every record, without an
  attachment identity filter, at =e-board-runtime-admission.el:449-475=;
- attachment-scoped =retry= likewise collects all records from both Board
  buckets at =:478-530= and aborts every non-in-flight record;
- a secondary publication fault compounded with primary cleanup failure leaves
  the six-catalog vector =(0 0 0 1 0 0)=.  The admission-local field knows the
  record, but public attachment/Board retry has no catalog membership from
  which to discover it; and
- in a realistic post-Board/pre-runtime-completion window, retiring attachment
  A marks and aborts attachment B's distinct committed admission on the same
  Board.  B's Work relation is removed even though A's exact lifetime supplied
  the retirement authority.

The public primary-only witness continues through later successful enrollment,
exact attachment retirement, reattachment, and another enrollment.  Public
Work/invocation counts recover, but the orphan primary record remains.  This
separates the catalog leak from an ordinary service relation leak.

*** Why it matters

An attachment-local cleanup operation can destroy a sibling's valid work, and
a compounded supported fault can retain an unreachable runtime record for the
process lifetime.  The lower admission token also becomes coupled to a
higher-level attachment recovery implementation and cannot substitute without
carrying that private lifetime.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Keep the exact retry handle in a runtime-owned attachment/recovery object and
publish its object-local authority before fallible secondary indexes.  Filter
attachment fence/retry by the record's captured =eq= attachment even when a
Board bucket is used to recover a partial publication.  Provide a separately
named Board-wide recovery path for callers that intentionally own the whole
Board.  Remove the runtime-specific fields from lower admission tokens once
the runtime owner can discover every partial record.  Test two attachments on
one Board, primary-only compounded faults, replacements, later public
retire/re-ensure/submit, and exact catalog/lease/count cleanup.

** TODO Medium: Healthy settlement retains rollback objects and full root receipts

*** Expected

=F91-OWNER=, =F91-TOPOLOGY=, and the architecture contract require committed
Board-lifetime topology to retain compact link identity only.  Rollback
receipts, admission tokens, effect closures, and reciprocal semantic holders
must be released when their transaction reaches its terminal postcondition.

*** Observed

Direct 10,000-item event and index tests correctly show compact map entries and
zero full receipts.  The composed shape differs:

- =e-board-admission--finalize-event-node= derives a node's root at
  =e-board-admission.el:500-524=.  When a non-root sibling finalizes before its
  root receipt, its compact node retains that full root receipt; later root
  compaction does not retarget the sibling;
- =e-board--settlement-finish-admission= at =e-board.el:1415-1418= does not
  clear the invocation/aggregation settlement-token slot or activation
  event/effect receipt slots; and
- the effect drain marks a receipt removed at
  =e-board-admission.el:2848-2851= but retains its Board, queue cell, and effect
  closure fields.

A three-event same-admission completion leaves three compact nodes, two of
which retain a full root receipt.  A healthy 10,000-settlement run has zero
pending tokens in the Board catalog but still retains 10,000 semantic token
holders, 10,000 event-receipt holders, 10,000 effect-receipt holders, and
29,999 compact nodes pointing at full roots among 30,000 event nodes.

*** Why it matters

This is unbounded Board-lifetime memory retention.  The retained effect
closures keep semantic targets and Board objects reachable, while root
receipts retain rollback graphs.  It directly contradicts
=docs/architecture.md:216-222= and is not justified by later exact neighbour
repair.

*** Smallest corrective direction

Create or finalize the compact root before compacting its siblings, or
explicitly retarget every exact sibling root during root finalization.  After
terminal settlement completion, clear the invocation/aggregation admission
slot, activation receipt holders, and consumed effect receipt's Board/cell/
closure fields.  Add reachability/slot-shape tests for multi-event admissions
and healthy semantic settlements; checking only the map entry type is
insufficient.

* Architecture review

** Physical owners and dependency direction

The broad Feature 91 decomposition remains sound.  Chat presentation, session
values/storage, harness turn/context owners, OpenAI, MCP, base, and context
estimation each have a substantive owner and narrow facade.  The literal require
graph is 167/719 and acyclic; dependencies generally point from facades and
runtime orchestration toward stable state/admission/error contracts.

=e-board-runtime-error.el= is now independently loadable and correctly defines
the lower typed condition.  =e-board-runtime-admission.el= is also a real
concrete owner, not a generic transaction framework.  Its upward record stored
inside the lower =e-board-admission= token is nevertheless an ownership/lifetime
violation in the context of the sibling-scope and primary-only defects in
Finding 3.  The runtime facade itself uses public lower operations.  The Board
facade's two generated private receipt reads are the remaining concrete private
boundary leak.

** State, side effects, errors, and performance

Board event/routing/publication/pickup/settlement state is process-local and
correctly belongs to one Board lifetime.  Runtime attachment/catalog state is
also process-local and must remain exact to one attachment generation.  No
durable session/board association or wire format changed.

External invocation dispatch, scheduling, timer cancellation, event
publication, and observer callbacks remain at Board/runtime edges.  The
problem is not that those side effects need a new adapter; it is that Board
semantic state lacks a persistent stage after an irreversible callback.  The
smallest correction should remain concrete to settlement and admission rather
than introduce a speculative generic transaction abstraction.

Ordinary operations use exact maps, queues, and object-local buckets; no global
scan was introduced.  Direct 10,000-item event/index performance and compact
map shape are acceptable.  The 10,000 healthy settlement reachability result
is not: Board-lifetime token, receipt, closure, and full-root retention grows
linearly and has no causal justification.

Expected lower domain errors are typed and visible.  Findings 1 and 2 show two
remaining unexpected-error violations: a completion error selects the wrong
inverse, while a terminal diagnostic error can be swallowed and a prior
successful effect can be relabeled failed.

** Interfaces, substitutability, and compatibility

The new lower error and direct admission contracts substitute under fresh
load.  Runtime admission bucket operations are narrow and exact.  The lower
Board token should not be required to carry a runtime-specific recovery record,
and the facade should not require private =--removed-p= accessors.  Narrow
public completion/receipt status is the real missing contract; a broad receipt
interface is not warranted.

No durable or wire migration is required.  No new legacy fallback, id-only
receipt reconstruction, or private compatibility shim was found.  The changed
core record shapes intentionally require a restart.  Obsolete runtime-specific
lower token fields and private facade reads can be removed once their owner
contracts are corrected.

* Design self-check

** 1. If this work stopped here, is the project better off?

Yes, in architecture and direct recovery coverage: the lower error contract,
compact finalizers, and runtime recovery owner are materially stronger than the
prior checkpoint.  It is not acceptance-ready because the five actionable
findings include core settlement and recovery defects.

** 2. What final direction does it move toward?

One semantic owner per Board/runtime lifetime, exact resumable mutation
authority, narrow public facade operations, bounded committed state, and
provider/presentation-independent harness policy.

** 3. Are changing or uncertain decisions local and reversible?

Provider capability remains explicitly unconfirmed and outside this package.
The stable requirement is exact admission/settlement behavior; it should be
modeled directly.  The runtime recovery handle and settlement stage should
remain concrete local records, not speculative generic extension points.

** 4. What component owns each behavior, and is it cohesive?

Board admission owns exact low-level receipts and inverses; Board owns semantic
settlement/event order; runtime admission owns attachment-scoped recovery
catalogs; runtime owns enrollment/retirement orchestration.  Findings 1-3 are
precisely the points where disposition or lifetime crosses those owners without
an adequate public contract.

** 5. Does coupling increase or decrease, and are interfaces narrow?

Feature 91 reduces coupling overall.  This candidate's lower-token
=runtime-admission-record= field increases semantic coupling, and two Board
facade private reads bypass the intended narrow contract.  Both can be replaced
by owner-shaped exact status/recovery operations.

** 6. Did a dependency start pointing the wrong way?

No literal require edge points upward and the graph remains acyclic.  The
runtime-specific record stored in the lower Board token is a semantic wrong-way
state dependency despite the absence of a require edge.  Generated private
receipt reads also cross the facade/owner boundary.

** 7. Could side effects move outward?

The dispatcher, scheduler, timer, observer, and event-publication effects are
already at Board/runtime edges.  Moving them farther out would split atomic
ordering.  The needed change is durable process-local stage acknowledgement
around those effects, not another adapter.

** 8. What state is added or mutated, who owns it, and is its lifetime minimal?

All new admission/catalog/receipt state is process-local.  Exact compact link
nodes correctly live for the Board/queue lifetime.  Runtime records should live
for one exact attachment admission, not in a lower Board token or a sibling's
Board-wide cleanup scope.  Settlement receipts/tokens/closures should end at
terminal acknowledgement; Finding 4 shows they currently live too long.

** 9. What is the performance impact?

Normal paths remain indexed and direct 10,000-item operations are bounded.  No
global scan was found.  The linear retained object graph after healthy
settlement is an unjustified memory regression and must be removed; no domain
requirement needs those rollback objects after terminal completion.

** 10. Are abstractions real and substitutable?

The lower runtime error, Board admission, runtime admission, and public Board
facade are real concrete contracts.  Fresh-load error substitutability passes.
Requiring every lower admission token to carry an upper runtime record, and
requiring facade code to inspect private receipt flags, weakens substitution and
must be corrected with narrow real operations.

** 11. What compatibility expectations apply?

Public commands, durable session/Board formats, event ordering, and provider
request behavior must remain unchanged.  The candidate changes only
process-local structs and intentionally requires restart.  No id-only fallback
or compatibility shim is justified.

** 12. What legacy code can be removed now?

After remediation, remove the runtime-specific fields from Board admission
tokens, the two facade-private receipt accesses, and any stale scanner groups or
documentation totals.  Earlier receiptless/id-only compatibility paths remain
removed.

** 13. Where are expected and unexpected errors handled?

Expected owner errors use typed Board/runtime conditions and now fresh-load
correctly.  Unexpected completion/publication faults should remain visible with
their exact continuation.  Finding 1 selects an unsafe inverse; Finding 2 can
swallow a diagnostic error or overwrite a successful effect's meaning.  Those
paths do not yet satisfy the error contract.

** 14. What tests prove the core independently?

Direct Board-admission, runtime-admission-owner, Board, runtime, registry, Work,
and composition roots prove the normal owner contracts independently.  Full
fixture ERT proves broad preservation.  The 14 independent witnesses show that
current checked-in tests omit composed completion direction, terminal semantic
stage faults/cancellation, sibling-scope recovery, primary-only compounded
recovery, and semantic reachability.  Those cases can be added narrowly without
rewriting unrelated suites.
